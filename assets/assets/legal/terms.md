## 7. Shared Groups

The person who creates a Group becomes an Owner. A Group may have more than one Owner. Owners can manage invitations, join requests, membership, participant weights, and ownership transfer. Owners are not agents, debt collectors, trustees, or independent arbiters for other Members.

An invite link may be opened by anyone who receives it. A valid invite creates or reuses a pending request; an Owner must approve it before the applicant becomes an active Member and can view Group financial information.

Active Members can see Group information, participant labels, Shared Transactions, allocations, calculated balances, Settlements, and relevant history. Calculations are based on user-supplied Content and recorded allocation rules. They are not legal determinations of debt, bank statements, certified accounting records, payment instructions, or evidence that money moved.

A Settlement records a proposed or requested amount. Relevant users can confirm, reject, or cancel it. A confirmed Settlement changes Blenvy’s informational calculation but does not cause a transfer. “Payment proof” is a link to an existing transaction, not verification by Blenvy that payment occurred.

Leaving or removal ends general Group access, clears the active Group nickname, and preserves the minimum historical participation required for existing balances and records. It does not delete the person’s Account or private finances. A Member involved in a pending Settlement must first resolve or cancel it, and the last Owner must transfer ownership before leaving.

On completed Account deletion, Blenvy removes the user’s identity and private records. Where deleting a Shared Transaction, allocation, or completed Settlement would corrupt another Member’s history, Blenvy may retain only the minimum identity-stripped record needed for the calculation, such as dates, amounts, currencies, allocations, and Settlement roles. Descriptions, notes, categories, import details, profile information, and the original user identifier are removed. Other Members may nevertheless infer who a record concerned from context.
